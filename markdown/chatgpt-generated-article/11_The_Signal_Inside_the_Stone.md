# 11. The Signal Inside the Stone（石头里的信号）

**主题：** 太空探索 / 学校教育　 **目标词：** 41

At the university **observatory**, student Lina watched a probe complete a **flyby** of asteroid Orpheus. It was the final stage of a seven-year **expedition** in planetary **exploration**. On the screen, the **curve** of the asteroid’s trajectory crossed the **ecliptic** and settled into a temporary **orbit**. Its measured **diameter** and **radius** matched predictions, but its blue-green **spectrum** did not. What the public saw as a beautiful **spectacle** might conceal an unknown **substance**.

The probe returned a sealed **sample**. Lina’s **primary** task was to describe its **composition** without damaging the only **specimen**. She identified a **solid** outer shell and a pocket of **fluid** inside. Every **particle**, **molecule**, **ion**, and **electron** produced a **distinct** signal at a certain **frequency**. Through **ultraviolet** light and careful **refraction**, she found a barely **discernible** pattern surrounding an otherwise **invisible** carbon **compound**. Her professor wondered whether it was a **fossil** or a natural **formation** created by **quantum** effects. Lina proposed that the team **synthesize** the compound before opening the sample.

Then a storm damaged the roof **antenna** and overloaded a laboratory **circuit**. A falling instrument struck the container. Lina caught it, but one **fragment** broke away and a heavy cabinet nearly **squashed** the analysis unit. The possible loss felt **overwhelming**; years of work seemed one accident away from **despair**. She shut off the power and placed the fragment in a backup chamber.

Her caution saved the evidence. Later analysis showed that the pattern came from a **collision** between mineral-rich ice and solar radiation, not from life. It was still a major discovery: the same reaction might help explain how complex carbon compounds formed before a **cataclysmic** impact shaped the early planets. Lina did not find an alien fossil, but she learned that science advances when disappointment is examined as carefully as wonder.

## 中文翻译

在大学**天文台**里，学生莉娜注视着探测器完成对俄耳甫斯小行星的**飞越**。这是历时七年的行星**探索****远征**的最后阶段。屏幕上，小行星的**曲线**轮廓穿过**黄道**，进入临时**轨道**。测得的**直径**与**半径**都符合预测，但它呈现的蓝绿色**光谱**却不吻合。公众眼中的美丽**奇观**，可能隐藏着一种未知**物质**。

探测器送回一份密封**样本**。莉娜的**首要的**任务，是在不损坏唯一**标本**的情况下说明它的**组成**。她识别出**固体**外壳和内部一小袋**流体**。每个**粒子**、**分子**、**离子**和**电子**都会在特定**频率**产生**独特的**信号。借助**紫外线的**光和精确的**折射**分析，她发现一种勉强**可辨认的**图案，环绕着原本**不可见的**碳**化合物**。教授猜测它究竟是**化石**，还是由**量子的**效应产生的天然**构造**。莉娜建议，先在实验室**合成**这种化合物，再打开样本。

这时，一场暴风雨损坏了屋顶**天线**，并使实验室**电路**过载。一台掉落的仪器撞上容器。莉娜虽然接住了它，却仍有一块**碎片**脱落，一个沉重柜子还差点**压扁**分析设备。可能失去成果的压力令人**难以承受的**；多年工作仿佛只差一次意外就会化为**绝望**。她切断电源，把碎片放入备用舱。

她的谨慎保住了证据。后续分析表明，该图案源自富含矿物质的冰与太阳辐射之间的一次**碰撞**，而非生命迹象。这仍是一项重大发现：同样的反应或许能解释，在一次塑造早期行星的**灾变性的**撞击之前，复杂碳化合物如何形成。莉娜没有找到外星化石，却明白了一个道理：失望和惊奇一样，都值得科学认真审视。
