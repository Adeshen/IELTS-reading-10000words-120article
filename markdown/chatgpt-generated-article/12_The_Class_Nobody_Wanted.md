# 12. The Class Nobody Wanted（没人愿意教的班）

**主题：** 学校教育　 **目标词：** 41

Mira was a **novice** teacher when the **headmaster** of Northbridge **Academy** asked her to lead an evening class for adults who had never completed **secondary** school. The academy enjoyed great **prestige**, but this programme was **notorious** for losing students. Mira had little **expertise** in adult education, yet the headmaster chose her because she was **diligent**, patient, and **capable** of listening. Her first task was to **recruit** twelve learners affected by **illiteracy** and help them become both **literate** and **numerate**.

The central **issue** soon appeared. Years of humiliation had convinced the students that education belonged to an **elite**. Tomas, a **clever** mechanic with strong practical **aptitude**, called himself an **idiot** whenever a textbook passage seemed **obscure**. Salma could solve a market-price **riddle** with quick **wit**, but froze before written arithmetic. Mira refused to make them **cram** rules. Her **scheme** used bus schedules, repair manuals, and household bills to **instil** confidence. She would **emphasise** one useful skill each week, then **stimulate** discussion through real problems.

An **eminent** professor visited and dismissed the lessons as too simple. His criticism almost **spoiled** the class’s new confidence. Mira asked each learner to explain a skill from work instead. Tomas demonstrated an engine fault; Salma calculated interest; a cook described food safety. Their practical **wisdom** revealed an **all-round** intelligence no examination had measured. The professor admitted that being academically **intelligent**, socially **smart**, or mechanically gifted were different strengths; no single test could identify a **genius**.

That recognition gave the class fresh **impetus**. Their personal **motive** was no longer merely a certificate: they wanted to **enhance** their independence. Mira did not **indulge** excuses, but she used every small success to **motivate** them and **spur** further effort. After months of **painstaking** work, all twelve passed. At graduation, they received the loudest applause and the **esteem** they had once believed was reserved for others.

## 中文翻译

米拉还是一名**新手**教师时，北桥**学院**的**校长**请她负责一个成人夜校班，学生都没有完成**中等的**学校教育。学院享有很高的**声望**，这个项目却因学生不断退出而**声名狼藉的**。米拉缺少成人教育方面的**专业知识**，校长仍选择她，因为她十分**勤奋的**、有耐心，而且**有能力的**倾听别人。她的首项任务是**招募**十二名受**文盲问题**影响的学员，帮助他们既能**识字的**，也能**识数的**。

核心**问题**很快显现出来。多年的羞辱让学生认定，教育只属于**精英**。托马斯是一名**聪明的**机械师，具有很强的实践**天资**，可每当教材内容显得**晦涩的**，他就骂自己是**笨蛋**。萨尔玛能凭敏捷的**机智**解开市场价格**谜题**，面对书面算术却会僵住。米拉不让他们**死记硬背**规则。她的教学**方案**使用公交时刻表、维修手册和家庭账单来**逐渐灌输**信心。她每周**强调**一种实用技能，再用真实问题**激发**讨论。

一位**杰出的**教授前来参观，却嫌课程过于简单。他的批评险些**破坏**全班刚建立的信心。米拉转而请每位学员讲解自己的工作技能。托马斯演示发动机故障，萨尔玛计算利息，一名厨师讲解食品安全。他们的实践**智慧**展现了考试从未衡量过的**全面的**才智。教授承认，学术上**有才智的**、社交上**机灵的**和机械方面有天赋，是不同的长处；没有任何一种考试能够认定谁是**天才**。

这份认可给全班带来了新的**推动力**。他们学习的个人**动机**不再只是一张证书，而是希望**增强**自己的独立能力。米拉不会**纵容**借口，却用每一次小成功去**激励**他们，并**促使**他们继续努力。经过数月**艰苦细致的**学习，十二人全部通过考试。毕业典礼上，他们获得最热烈的掌声，也赢得了曾经以为只属于别人的**尊重**。
