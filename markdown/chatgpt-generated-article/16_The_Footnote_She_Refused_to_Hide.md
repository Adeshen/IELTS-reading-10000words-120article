# 16. The Footnote She Refused to Hide（她不肯隐瞒的脚注）

**主题：** 学校教育　 **目标词：** 41

At the **outset** of her final **semester**, engineering student Nia chose a **selective** **elective** on low-carbon concrete. The course **syllabus** made one field **assignment** a **prerequisite** for graduation. Nia began with only a **rudimentary**, **elementary** understanding of the **basic** principles of materials, but the wider **curriculum** had taught her scientific **discipline**. Her goal was to **compose** a new **formula** whose every **component** would **blend** waste glass with cement.

Her early results looked **profound** on the **surface**, yet she knew an **empirical** claim required evidence. She would **inspect** each block, **scrutinise** every crack, and **extract** strength data before making a **point**. Her supervisor would not **dictate** a conclusion or let any engineering **doctrine** replace observation. At a student **forum**, Nia used a short **preview** to **introduce** the project and **elicit** questions. The audience understood the **gist**, but one listener asked where she had found a striking sentence under the second **heading**.

Nia discovered that she had copied the sentence into an early **draft** without quotation marks. She had never meant to **plagiarise**, but intention did not erase the error. Some classmates urged her to delete it before the final **review** and **presume** nobody would notice. Instead, she found the original **reference**, chose to **cite** its author, and used a brief **quote** only where necessary. She then rewrote the surrounding **paper** in her own words and asked the library to **print** a similarity report.

Correcting the mistake delayed her **dissertation** and forced her to **revise** two chapters. Yet the transparent account strengthened her research. Her final tests showed that the glass mixture reduced waste without weakening the concrete. At graduation, Nia’s supervisor praised not only the formula but her decision to expose an uncomfortable footnote. A sound result, Nia learned, depended on honest scholarship as surely as a building depended on its foundations.

## 中文翻译

在最后一个**学期**的**开始**，工程专业学生妮娅选择了一门关于低碳混凝土的**选择性的****选修课**。课程**教学大纲**规定，一项实地**作业**是毕业的**先决条件**。妮娅起初对材料的**基本的**原理只有**初步的**、**基础阶段的**理解，但更广泛的**课程体系**已经培养了她的科学**严谨作风**。她的目标是**构成**一种新**配方**，让其中每一种**组成部分**都能把废玻璃与水泥**混合**起来。

早期结果从**表面**看似乎十分**深刻的**，妮娅却明白，任何**以实验为依据的**结论都需要证据。她会**检查**每块试件，**仔细审查**每条裂缝，并**提取**强度数据后再提出**要点**。导师不会**口授命令**一个结论，也不会让任何工程**教条**取代观察。在学生**论坛**上，妮娅先用简短**预告**来**介绍**项目并**引出**问题。听众理解了内容**主旨**，但有人问她，第二个**标题**下那句醒目的话来自哪里。

妮娅发现，自己曾把那句话抄进早期**草稿**，却忘了加引号。她从未有意**剽窃**，但没有恶意并不能消除错误。有些同学劝她在最终**审查**前删掉那句话，并**假定**无人会发现。她反而找出原始**参考资料**，决定**引用**作者，只在必要处保留一句简短**引文**。随后，她用自己的语言重写周围的**论文**内容，并请图书馆**打印**相似度报告。

纠正错误耽误了她的**学位论文**，也迫使她**修改**两个章节。然而，坦诚的说明反而增强了研究的可信度。最终测试表明，玻璃混合物能够减少废料，又不会削弱混凝土。毕业时，导师称赞的不只是配方，还有她主动公开那条令人尴尬脚注的决定。妮娅明白，可靠成果离不开诚实学术，就像建筑离不开地基一样。
