# 17. The Question Behind the Headaches（头痛背后的问题）

**主题：** 学校教育　 **目标词：** 41

When students in Room 204 began reporting headaches, most teachers **supposed** that exam anxiety was responsible. Science student Ravi proposed a different **hypothesis**: poor ventilation. He did not **postulate** that the answer was certain or **speculate** about blame. Instead, he asked the nurse to help **diagnose** symptoms and designed a **survey** to **inquire** when they appeared. One anonymous **query** asked whether students could **perceive** any smell before becoming **conscious** of pain.

Ravi began to **observe** the room at different hours. A sensor could **detect** carbon dioxide, but he also had to **discern** patterns in human reports. He would **compare** morning and afternoon classes, **contrast** Room 204 with another room, and mark where their schedules **overlapped**. One apparent **contradiction** mattered: headaches were worse on cool days, although heat had seemed the likely cause. Ravi noted every **detail** and treated each **instance** as evidence rather than proof.

Using **inductive** reasoning, he could **infer** that closed windows increased the risk and **deduce** that a blocked air duct intensified it. The data seemed to **imply** a connection, yet the principal **deemed** the sample too small. Ravi could **comprehend** that caution, but he continued to **insist** on an inspection and **persist** despite a low project **grade**. His model used the **analogy** of lungs to **illustrate** airflow and a smoke test to **demonstrate** the blockage. The fault finally became **manifest** when dust blew back into the room.

The school received a safety **grant** and repaired the duct. Students’ **feedback** confirmed that the headaches stopped. Ravi did not **reckon** he had proved every cause, nor did he **underestimate** the **nuance** of stress and sleep. After he **deliberated** carefully, he could only **conclude** that ventilation had been the main factor. That modest conclusion helped **resolve** the problem—and **achieve** more than an impressive but unsupported claim ever could.

## 中文翻译

204教室的学生开始头痛时，多数老师都**假设**原因是考试焦虑。理科生拉维提出另一项**假设**：通风不良。他没有**假定**答案已经确定，也没有**推测**该责怪谁，而是请校医协助**诊断**症状，并设计一份**调查**，**询问**症状何时出现。其中一个匿名**问题**问道，学生在**意识到**疼痛之前，是否能**察觉**某种气味。

拉维开始在不同时段**观察**教室。传感器可以**检测**二氧化碳，他还必须从学生报告中**辨别**规律。他会**比较**上午与下午的课堂，将204教室和另一间教室作**对照**，并标出课程时间发生**重叠**的地方。有一个明显的**矛盾**很重要：天气凉爽时头痛反而更严重，尽管高温原本似乎最可疑。拉维记录每个**细节**，把每个**事例**视为证据而非定论。

利用**归纳的**推理，他可以**推断**关窗会增加风险，并进一步**演绎推理**出堵塞的风道会加剧问题。数据似乎**暗示**两者有关，校长却**认为**样本太小。拉维能够**理解**这种谨慎，但仍**坚持要求**检查，并在项目**成绩**很低的情况下继续**坚持**。他的模型用肺作**类比**来**说明**气流，又通过烟雾测试**证明**堵塞。灰尘被吹回教室时，故障终于清楚地**显现**出来。

学校获得一笔安全**拨款**，修好了风道。学生的**反馈**证实头痛已经停止。拉维并不**认为**自己证明了所有病因，也没有**低估**压力与睡眠之间的微妙**细微差别**。经过认真**深思熟虑**，他只能**得出结论**：通风不良是主要因素。这个克制的结论帮助学校**解决**问题，并**取得**了夸张却缺乏依据的主张永远无法取得的成果。
