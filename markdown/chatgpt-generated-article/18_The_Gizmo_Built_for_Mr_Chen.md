# 18. The Gizmo Built for Mr Chen（为陈师傅造的小装置）

**主题：** 学校教育 / 科技发明　 **目标词：** 40

Lena, a student **engineer** at Eastport **Polytechnic**, applied for a design **fellowship**. She wanted to **invent** a workshop assistant for Mr Chen, a talented **mechanic** whose eyesight was failing. Commercial systems cost too much and **overestimated** what a small garage needed. Lena’s **foundation** was simple: build one **versatile** **device** that could read labels, locate tools, and warn of moving machinery.

She began to **devise** experiments in the college fabrication **facility**. A tiny **microcomputer** formed the core, with a **keyboard** and high-contrast **screen** as an **auxiliary** control. For **optics**, Lena fitted a wide **lens**; a **radar** unit measured motion, while an **echo** **sensor** detected nearby walls. The software used a secure **browser** and **multimedia** instructions. Lena learned to **specialise** in accessible design without narrowing the machine’s uses. She could **utilise** cheap recycled plastic, a harmless **byproduct** from a local factory, for each protective **accessory**.

Mr Chen became the first **tester**. He called the prototype a clever **gizmo**, then immediately **discovered** its biggest flaw: the voice counted every hanging spanner twice. Lena had to **count** the tools by hand, **estimate** the error, **compute** a correction, and **gauge** whether the warning arrived quickly enough. Rather than hide the failure, she chose to **disclose** it in her fellowship interview. Her test records **revealed** that polished metal confused both camera and radar and **uncovered** a safer solution: coloured rubber sleeves.

Lena learned that to **innovate** was not merely to **assemble** impressive parts. It meant entering another person’s working **domain** and understanding the human **realm** behind a technical problem. She won the fellowship, but her real success came when Mr Chen used the finished device to repair a bicycle independently. He handed it back only once—to add his fingerprint beside hers on the design certificate.

## 中文翻译

东港**理工学院**的学生**工程师**莱娜申请了一项设计**研究奖学金**。她想为视力日渐衰退的优秀**机械师**陈师傅**发明**一台车间助手。商业系统价格高昂，而且**高估**了小型修理铺的实际需求。莱娜的设计**基础**很简单：制造一台**多用途的****设备**，既能读取标签、寻找工具，也能提醒使用者注意运转中的机器。

她开始在学院制造**设施**里**设计**实验。一台小型**微型计算机**构成核心，配有**键盘**和高对比度**屏幕**作为**辅助的**控制装置。在**光学**方面，莱娜安装一枚广角**镜头**；**雷达**装置测量运动，**回声****传感器**则探测附近墙壁。软件使用安全**浏览器**和**多媒体**说明。莱娜学会**专门研究**无障碍设计，又没有限制机器的用途。她还可以**利用**当地工厂无害的塑料**副产品**，制作每一个保护性**配件**。

陈师傅成为第一位**测试者**。他把原型称为聪明的**小装置**，却立刻**发现**了最大缺陷：语音系统会把每把挂着的扳手数两遍。莱娜只好手工**计数**工具、**估计**误差、**计算**修正值，并**衡量**警报是否足够及时。她没有掩盖失败，而是选择在奖学金面试中**披露**。测试记录**揭示**了光亮金属会同时干扰摄像头和雷达，并**发现**了更安全的解决方案：彩色橡胶套。

莱娜明白，**创新**并不只是**组装**令人惊叹的零件。它意味着走进另一个人的工作**领域**，理解技术问题背后的人类**范围**。她获得了奖学金，但真正的成功发生在陈师傅独立使用成品修好一辆自行车时。他只把设备交还过一次——为了在设计证书上、她的指纹旁边按下自己的指纹。
