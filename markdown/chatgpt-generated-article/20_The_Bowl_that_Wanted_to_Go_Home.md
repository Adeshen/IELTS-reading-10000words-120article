# 20. The Bowl That Wanted to Go Home（想回家的陶碗）

**主题：** 文化历史　 **目标词：** 40

Curator Leo was preparing his **museum**’s largest exhibition, a **chronology** of belief and power. One gallery explained **feudalism** through the seals of a **lord**, though the centrepiece was an **imperial** crown said to have passed from an **emperor** to a **king**, then a **queen**, **prince**, and **princess**. Labels addressed each ruler as “Your **Majesty**.” Another room traced an ancient **empire** through a Buddhist **pagoda**, a Christian **church** and **cathedral**, and portraits of a **Pope**, **bishop**, **priest**, **monk**, and travelling **missionary** carrying a **Bible**. A recording of a children’s **choir** filled the hall.

The final object was a piece of painted **pottery**. An old catalogue called it “**exotic** decoration made by an **aboriginal** **tribe**,” language that treated its maker as almost **alien**. **Anthropologist** Mara, herself an **inhabitant** of the island where it had been found, challenged the display. Her grandmother said the bowl held the community’s **soul** and belonged to a **sacred** burial **ritual**. Its rim was **engraved** with names spoken only on **hallowed** ground.

Leo argued that his grandfather had helped **excavate** the site and that he would legally **inherit** the collection. Mara replied that **archaeology** did not erase responsibility. Returning the bowl was an **ethical** obligation, not a favour, and the museum’s **humanitarian** mission meant respecting living people as well as dead rulers. Leo feared losing the exhibition’s star object, but he invited island elders to examine the records and follow their own **etiquette** around the bowl.

The evidence showed it had been removed without consent. Leo returned it and replaced the empty case with a video made by the community. Visitors saw the bowl welcomed home and learned why cultural **heritage** could not always be owned by whoever held the key to a storeroom. The exhibition became more powerful because one object was absent: its empty space told the truth.

## 中文翻译

策展人利奥正为他的**博物馆**筹备规模最大的展览，以**年代顺序**呈现信仰与权力。一间展厅通过**领主**的印章解释**封建制度**，中心展品则是一顶**皇家的**王冠，据说曾从一位**皇帝**手中传给一位**国王**，又传给**女王**、**王子**和**公主**。说明牌一律以“您的**陛下**”称呼统治者。另一展厅通过一座佛教**宝塔**、一座基督教**教堂**和一座**大教堂**，以及**教皇**、**主教**、**神父**、**僧侣**和携带**《圣经》**的旅行**传教士**画像，讲述一个古老**帝国**。儿童**唱诗班**的录音回荡在展厅中。

最后一件展品是一只彩绘**陶器**。旧目录称它是“某个**土著的****部落**制作的**异域的**装饰品”，这种语言几乎把制作者当成**陌生异类**。**人类学家**玛拉本身就是陶碗出土地岛屿的**居民**，她对这种展示提出质疑。祖母告诉她，这只碗承载着族群的**灵魂**，属于一项**神圣的**葬礼**仪式**。碗沿**雕刻**着一些名字，只有在**圣洁的**土地上才能念出。

利奥辩称，祖父曾协助**发掘**遗址，自己也将依法**继承**这批藏品。玛拉回答，**考古学**不能抹去责任。归还陶碗是一项**合乎伦理的**义务，而非恩惠；博物馆的**人道主义的**使命意味着，既要尊重死去的统治者，也要尊重仍然生活着的人。利奥担心失去展览的明星展品，但还是邀请岛上长者查阅记录，并按照他们自己的**礼仪**接近陶碗。

证据显示，陶碗当年未经同意便被带走。利奥归还了它，用族群制作的视频取代空展柜。参观者看到陶碗回归故乡，也明白了为什么文化**遗产**并不总该属于那个掌握库房钥匙的人。展览因为一件物品的缺席而更有力量：空出来的位置讲出了真相。
