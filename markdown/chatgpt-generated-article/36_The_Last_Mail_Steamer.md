# 36. The Last Mail Steamer（最后一班邮轮）

**主题：** 交通旅行／国家政府　 **目标词：** 40

At dawn, Mara was due to **embark** on a passenger **liner**, but a radio message changed her plan. A small mail **steamer** had struck a hidden **hazard** outside the harbour **channel**. Its elderly **seaman** reported that the **vessel** might soon **submerge**. Mara, the harbour engineer, ran along the **dock** and ordered a rescue boat to lift its **anchor**. On the wet **deck**, she checked the engine's **turbine** and **propeller**, while two volunteers tied a **raft** and a **canoe** to the **stern**. They also loaded an extra **oar** in case the motor failed.

The rescue had to **depart** before the morning **lull** in the storm **expired**. However, a freight **locomotive** had derailed beside the **railroad**, blocking the road to the fuel depot. Workers crossed a drainage **ditch**, entered an old **underground** maintenance **tunnel**, and carried fuel cans through a closed **subway** station. The accident caused a serious **delay**. Officials suggested they **postpone** the mission or **defer** it until daylight, but Mara knew that waiting would **speed** the damaged ship's sinking. Its downward **velocity** was already increasing.

When the rescue boat reached the steamer, Mara found more than sailors aboard. The hold contained every **package** of postal ballots for an island election. Each sack bore an official **stamp**, and the captain had promised to deliver the **mail** before noon. The waterlogged cargo was a heavy **burden**, so the crews used ropes to **transfer** it onto the raft. Mara then used a portable antenna to **transmit** the vote records ashore while the physical ballots remained in **transit**.

The election mattered because the island council was debating **socialism** after years of studying **Marxism**. Mara supported neither side publicly; she cared that citizens, not the sea, should decide. Minutes after everyone climbed aboard the rescue boat, the steamer disappeared beneath the waves. The ballots arrived late but intact, and the council extended the deadline. For Mara, the successful rescue proved that transport was not merely about machines: sometimes a working route protected democracy itself.

## 中文翻译

黎明时分，玛拉原本准备**登上**一艘客运**班轮**，但一则无线电消息改变了她的计划。一艘小型邮政**汽船**撞上了港口**航道**外一处隐蔽的**危险物**。船上一名年长的**海员**报告说，这艘**船只**很快可能会**沉入水中**。身为港口工程师的玛拉沿着**码头**跑去，命令救援艇起**锚**。她在湿滑的**甲板**上检查发动机的**涡轮机**和**螺旋桨**，两名志愿者则把一只**木筏**和一艘**独木舟**系在**船尾**，并装上一支备用**船桨**，以防发动机失灵。

救援艇必须在风暴早晨的短暂**平静期****结束**前**出发**。然而，一辆货运**机车**在**铁路**旁脱轨，堵住了通往燃料库的道路。工人越过一条排水**沟**，进入旧的**地下**维修**隧道**，又穿过一座关闭的**地铁**站搬运燃料罐。这场事故造成了严重**延误**。官员建议**推迟**任务，或者把行动**延期**至天亮，但玛拉知道，等待会**加速**受损船只下沉，其下降**速度**已经越来越快。

救援艇抵达汽船时，玛拉发现船上不只有水手。货舱中装着海岛选举的每一件邮寄选票**包裹**。每个邮袋都有官方**邮戳**，船长曾承诺在中午前送达这些**邮件**。浸水的货物成了沉重的**负担**，船员便用绳索将其**转移**到木筏上。随后，玛拉用便携式天线把投票记录**传送**到岸上，而纸质选票仍在**运输途中**。

这次选举十分重要，因为岛上议会在研究多年**马克思主义**后，正就**社会主义**展开辩论。玛拉没有公开支持任何一方；她在意的是，决定应由公民作出，而不是由大海替他们决定。所有人登上救援艇几分钟后，汽船便没入海浪。选票虽然迟到，却完好无损，议会也延长了截止时间。对玛拉而言，这场成功的救援证明，交通运输不只是机器运转；有时，一条畅通的路线本身也在守护民主。
