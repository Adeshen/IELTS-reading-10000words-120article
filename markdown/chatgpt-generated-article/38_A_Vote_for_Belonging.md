# 38. A Vote for Belonging（关于归属的一票）

**主题：** 国家政府　 **目标词：** 40

The Hill House had once provided temporary **asylum** to refugees; now its diverse **population** wanted it to become a permanent community centre. A **demographic** survey showed residents from several **ethnic** groups: a **Jewish** baker, an **Arabian** nurse, a **Portuguese** carpenter, and families with **Roman**, **Spanish**, **Swiss**, **Greek**, **Italian**, and other **European** roots. An elderly teacher even kept his grandfather's **Soviet** passport beside a book of **Latin** poems. They were not one **clan**, but they had learned to live in **harmony**.

When the council offered residents a voting **franchise**, every adult was **entitled** to choose the building's future. Mira, the youngest **respondent** in an earlier **poll**, was **nominated** to organise the ballot. Her practical **checklist** covered registration, wheelchair access, translators, and a clear rule of **succession** if the elected chair resigned. The council's **guideline** required political neutrality, while Mira added one personal **ethic**: no speaker could insult another person's origin.

Two plans emerged. A developer's **proposal** promised luxury flats and gave a strong **hint** of profit. The residents' plan would **implement** language classes, childcare, and a clinic to improve everyone's **well-being**. From the developer's **perspective**, the old house was wasted property. His lawyer expressed the same **viewpoint**, while the council's financial **standpoint** remained cautious. Mira's **outlook** was more hopeful: with **steady** support, she believed local businesses would **flourish**, giving the district a better **prospect** than another gated tower.

On voting day, independent observers came to **monitor** the boxes. When the count ended, Mira stood on the steps to **proclaim** that the community plan had won. The mayor rose to **affirm** the result. Some residents cried, not because a building had been saved, but because their equal right to decide had finally been recognised. Months later, Hill House opened its clinic and classrooms. Its many languages still filled the corridors, yet difference no longer seemed like evidence that its residents belonged elsewhere; it was proof of what they had built together.

## 中文翻译

希尔之家过去曾为难民提供临时**庇护**；如今，这里多元的**人口**希望把它改成永久社区中心。一项**人口统计的**调查显示，居民来自多个**族群的**背景：有一位**犹太人**面包师、一位**阿拉伯半岛的**护士、一位**葡萄牙的**木匠，还有祖籍属于**罗马的**、**西班牙的**、**瑞士的**、**希腊的**、**意大利的**及其他**欧洲的**家庭。一位年长教师甚至把祖父的**苏联的**护照和一本**拉丁语的**诗集合放在一起。他们并非同一个**家族**，却学会了**和谐**相处。

市议会赋予居民投票**选举权**后，每个成年人都**有权**选择这座建筑的未来。在先前一次**民意调查**中，米拉是最年轻的**受访者**，随后又被**提名**负责组织投票。她那张务实的**核对清单**涵盖选民登记、无障碍通道、翻译人员，以及当选主席辞职时明确的**继任**规则。市议会的**指导原则**要求保持政治中立，米拉又加入了一条个人**道德准则**：任何发言者都不得侮辱他人的出身。

最终出现了两个方案。开发商的**提议**承诺建造豪华公寓，并强烈**暗示**项目能够获利。居民方案则会**实施**语言课程、儿童照护和诊所，以改善每个人的**福祉**。从开发商的**视角**看，这座老房子是被浪费的地产；他的律师表达了同样的**观点**，而市议会从财政**立场**出发，仍然保持谨慎。米拉的**看法**更加乐观：她相信，只要获得**稳定的**支持，本地企业就会**蓬勃发展**，让这个街区拥有比另一座封闭式高楼更好的**前景**。

投票当天，独立观察员前来**监督**票箱。计票结束后，米拉站在台阶上**宣布**社区方案获胜，市长随后起身**确认**结果。一些居民流下眼泪，并不只是因为一座建筑得以保留，更因为他们平等作出决定的权利终于得到承认。几个月后，希尔之家开设了诊所和教室。走廊里依然回响着多种语言，但差异不再说明这里的居民属于别处，而成为他们共同建设家园的证明。
