# 39. The Grocer Who Kept the Lights On（让店铺灯火不灭的杂货商）

**主题：** 国家政府／社会经济　 **目标词：** 40

Arun, an **Indian** **merchant**, ran a neighbourhood **retail** **grocery** in Melbourne, **Australia**. His shelves reflected years of international **trade**: honey from **New Zealand**, tea from **Britain**, jam from **France**, and biscuits from **Germany**. Then a global **financial** **crisis** struck. The local **economy** entered a **recession**, wholesale prices rose with **inflation**, and customers began to **spend** less. Arun's daily **revenue** and **gross** sales started to **dwindle**, leaving a growing budget **deficit**.

His latest shipment brought an unpleasant surprise. A new **tariff**, customs **duty**, and emergency port **levy** had all been **imposed** on imported food. The supplier's **invoice** exceeded what Arun could **afford**, although the delivery **receipt** showed fewer boxes than ordered. His landlord would not reduce the **lease**, and every ordinary business **expense** still had to be paid. Arun considered closing before falling prices and **deflation** made his remaining **market** even weaker.

Instead, he asked the neighbourhood for help. A local community **fund** agreed to **donate** a small grant, while a retired customer offered to **endow** a weekly free-meal programme. Arun launched a careful **promotion** rather than a desperate clearance **sale**: families could **exchange** a food-bank **voucher** for fresh produce, and paying customers received a **coupon** for their next visit. His handwritten **slogan** read, “Buy what you need; share what you can.” He also challenged the incorrect customs charge and recovered part of his **tax** payment.

The shop did not suddenly become rich, but it survived. Customers returned because the plan protected dignity: nobody had to explain whether they were buying food or receiving it. Arun learned that a shop's value could not be measured solely by profit. Months later, when the economy stabilised, he repaid the community fund and expanded the meal programme. The crisis had nearly emptied his shelves, yet it also revealed that honest exchange could turn a vulnerable business into a place of mutual support.

## 中文翻译

阿伦是一名**印度的****商人**，在**澳大利亚**墨尔本经营一家社区**零售的****杂货店**。他的货架体现着多年的国际**贸易**：有来自**新西兰**的蜂蜜、**英国**的茶、**法国**的果酱以及**德国**的饼干。随后，一场全球**金融的****危机**爆发。本地**经济**陷入**经济衰退**，批发价格随着**通货膨胀**上涨，顾客开始减少**花费**。阿伦每天的**收入**和**总的**销售额不断**缩减**，预算**赤字**也越来越大。

他最新收到的一批货又带来坏消息。新的**关税**、海关**税**和港口紧急**征费**全都被**强制征收**在进口食品上。供应商的**发票**金额超出了阿伦能够**负担**的范围，而送货**收据**显示实际箱数还少于订单数量。房东不肯降低**租约**费用，每一项日常经营**开支**仍须支付。阿伦一度考虑关店，因为他担心价格持续下跌及**通货紧缩**会使剩余**市场**更加疲弱。

但他转而向社区求助。本地社区**基金**同意**捐赠**一小笔补助金，一位退休顾客则提出**捐资支持**每周免费供餐计划。阿伦没有进行绝望式清仓，而是推出谨慎的**促销活动**：家庭可以用食物银行的**代金券****兑换**新鲜农产品，付费顾客则会收到下次使用的**优惠券**。他的手写**口号**是：“按需要购买，按能力分享。”他还对错误的海关收费提出申诉，追回了部分**税款**。

商店并未突然变得富有，却生存了下来。顾客愿意回来，是因为这个计划保护了人的尊严：没有人需要解释自己是在买食物，还是在接受援助。阿伦明白，商店的价值不能只用利润衡量。几个月后，经济趋于稳定，他偿还了社区基金，并扩大供餐计划。危机曾险些清空货架，却也证明，诚实的交换能够把一家脆弱的小店变成彼此扶持的地方。
