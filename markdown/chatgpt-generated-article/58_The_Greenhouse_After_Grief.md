# 58. The Greenhouse After Grief（悲伤之后的温室）

**主题：** 身心健康　 **目标词：** 40

After his wife's death, carpenter Emil lived in **agony**. **Grief** made ordinary days **miserable**, and he would **grieve** alone in his **private** workshop. A **gloomy** silence filled the house. Friends feared that repeated disappointment would **discourage** him, while Emil blamed **fate** and felt too **wretched** to answer their calls. Even a dripping tap became a terrible **nuisance** that could **irritate** him into **rage**. She also lived with constant **distress** after the loss.

His granddaughter Mina proposed rebuilding her grandmother's greenhouse. Emil's first **intuition** was to refuse. The work would be **strenuous** and require **rigorous** measurements; rotten beams were **liable** to fall. Mina's **naive**, sometimes **childish** confidence made him **frown**, but her **sincere** purpose softened him. Together they chose **sturdy** timber and worked through **temperate** spring days. Emil remained **steadfast**, teaching her to be careful, while Mina handled each fragile seedling with **tender** hands.

The project exposed their **mutual** pain. Mina said her grandfather seemed sane but absent; Emil admitted that even a **sane** person could suffer. Their sorrow did not **resemble** an **imaginary** illness or an **illusion**. His anger was real: he could become **furious**, **hate** cheerful music, and feel **hatred** toward the unfair world. Yet the greenhouse gave those emotions somewhere to go.

One morning, a **spontaneous** shower washed dust from the glass, leaving the young plants almost **naked** in sunlight. The beauty carried an **implicit** promise rather than a cure. Emil, once a cautious craftsman, became unexpectedly **enterprising** and planned to sell herbs for the hospice. The task remained partly **mundane** and often **frustrating**, but he proved **trustworthy** to every customer. He did not stop missing his wife; he simply built a life strong enough to hold both love and loss.

## 中文翻译

妻子去世后，木匠埃米尔一直生活在**痛苦**中。**悲伤**使普通日子变得**悲惨**，他总是独自在**私人的**工作间里**哀悼**。家中弥漫着**阴郁的**寂静。朋友担心接连的失望会使他**泄气**，埃米尔却责怪**命运**，觉得自己太过**可怜**，连电话也不愿接。甚至漏水的水龙头都会成为糟糕的**麻烦事**，足以**激怒**他，使他陷入**暴怒**。 失去亲人后，她还承受着持续的**忧伤**。

孙女米娜提议重建祖母的温室。埃米尔的第一**直觉**是拒绝。这项工作会很**费力**，还需要**严格的**测量；腐烂横梁很**可能**掉下来。米娜**天真的**、有时甚至**孩子气的**信心让他**皱眉**，但她**真诚的**心愿软化了他。他们共同选用**结实的**木材，在气候**温和的**春日里劳作。埃米尔始终**坚定不移**，教她谨慎做事；米娜则用**温柔的**双手照料每株脆弱幼苗。

这项工程暴露了两人**共同的**痛苦。米娜说，祖父看起来神志正常，心却不在这里；埃米尔承认，即使是**心智健全的**人也会受苦。他们的悲伤并不**类似于**某种**想象出来的**疾病，更不是**幻觉**。他的愤怒真实存在：他会变得**怒不可遏**，会**讨厌**欢快音乐，也会对不公平的世界充满**仇恨**。但温室让这些情绪有了安放之处。

一天清晨，一场**自发而来的**阵雨洗掉玻璃上的尘土，幼苗在阳光下几乎**裸露无掩**。这份美好带来一种**含蓄的**承诺，而非治愈一切的灵药。曾经谨慎的工匠埃米尔变得意外地**富有进取心**，计划出售香草，为临终关怀机构筹款。工作依然有些**平凡乏味**，也常常**令人沮丧**，但他向每一位顾客证明自己**值得信赖**。他并没有停止思念妻子，只是重新建起一种足以同时容纳爱与失去的生活。
